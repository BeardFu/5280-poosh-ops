Getting to the Race Track

🚕 Option 1: Uber/Taxi (RECOMMENDED)

Most convenient and reliable. Budget 150–250 MXN (~$8–14 USD) during non-peak hours. Peak hours (morning of race) may be 2–3x higher. Request rides early (aim to leave by 10:00–10:30 AM Denver / 11:00–11:30 AM local).

🚇 Option 2: Metro + Local Transport

Take Metro Line 2 southbound toward Tasqueña, exit at station closest to circuit. Then local bus or short taxi ride. Budget 1.5–2 hours total. Cheapest option (~20 MXN = $1 USD).

🚌 Option 3: Shuttle Services

F1 organizers offer shuttle buses from central locations. Check official F1 Mexico website or hotel concierge for details. Pre-book if available.

🎫 Ticket Tips

Arrive 2–3 hours early on race day. Bring: tickets (print or digital), ID, sunscreen, hat, comfortable shoes. Parking fills quickly if driving.

📱 What to Bring

Portable charger, water bottle, snacks, cash (some vendors don't take cards). Weather in early Nov: 15–25°C (59–77°F), mostly sunny.

🍽️ Food at Track

Track food is expensive. Eat beforehand or bring snacks. Beverages available but pricey. No outside food/drink allowed in some zones.

💡 Pro Tip: Book your return Uber/taxi the night before if possible. Hundreds of thousands of fans leave simultaneously, causing massive surge pricing and wait times.

💀 Día de los Muertos (Day of the Dead) Guide

🎉 Perfect Timing! November 1–2 is Día de los Muertos, one of Mexico's most important cultural celebrations. You'll experience this beautiful tradition firsthand during your trip. It's a colorful, vibrant celebration of life and remembrance—not dark or scary as outsiders sometimes think!

What is Día de los Muertos?

An ancient Mexican tradition blending indigenous and Catholic beliefs. Mexicans celebrate and honor deceased loved ones with ofrendas (altars), marigolds (cempasúchil flowers), pan de muerto (special bread), and calaveras (skull decorations).

When: October 31 – November 2 (peaks Nov 1–2)

Vibe: Joyful, colorful, spiritual—celebrating life rather than mourning death.

Expect vibrant marigold decorations, skeleton decorations, skull imagery, and temporary altars in parks, streets, and plazas. Take photos of the colorful displays. Many local shops and restaurants create elaborate decorations.

🍞 Traditional Foods to Try

Pan de Muerto (Bread of the Dead)
Available everywhere Oct 31 – Nov 2
Sweet bread shaped like a skull or round loaf with bone-like decorations. Tastes of orange and anise. Not spooky—delicious! Buy at bakeries, cafes, supermarkets. Perfect with hot chocolate or coffee for breakfast.
Must-TrySweet
Mole Negro (Black Mole)
Traditional Oaxacan dish, served during Día de Muertos
Complex, dark sauce with chocolate, chilies, and 20+ ingredients. Traditionally served over chicken. Labor-intensive to make. Many restaurants feature it this time of year. Try at traditional Mexican restaurants.
TraditionalComplex Flavor
Calaveras de Azúcar (Sugar Skulls)
Colorful candy skulls, available everywhere
Beautiful decorative skulls made from sugar. Often personalized with names on the forehead. Edible but often kept as decoration. Great souvenir. Buy from street vendors or candy shops throughout the city.
SouvenirCandy
Agua de Jamaica (Hibiscus Water)
Refreshing traditional drink
Tart, slightly sweet hibiscus tea served cold or hot. Iconic Día de Muertos beverage. You'll see it at festival events and street vendors. Try it!
BeverageRefreshing

🎨 Cultural Traditions to Experience

Face Painting — Calavera Makeup
Street artists and salons offer skull face painting Nov 1–2
Get your face painted in traditional skull/skeleton style (calavera). Beautiful, artistic designs. Street artists throughout city do this for 100–300 MXN. Great for photos and immersive experience.
Costumes & Dress
Skeleton/skull/Grim Reaper costumes common Nov 1–2
Many locals and tourists dress as skeletons or in Day of Dead themed outfits. Black with skull face paint is popular. Consider picking up a skeleton costume or skull mask at a local shop to participate in the festivities.
Marigold Markets
📍 Throughout city, concentrated near cemeteries and markets
Flower markets overflow with cempasúchil (bright orange marigolds) which symbolize death in Mexican tradition. Beautiful photo op. Buy a small bundle (30–50 MXN) as a souvenir or to participate in cemetery visits.

October 31 (Saturday) — Eve of Día de Muertos

Late evening: Walk through Roma/Condesa to see street decorations and temporary altars. Take photos of the vibrant setup.
November 1 (Sunday) — Día de Muertos + F1 RACE DAY ⚠️
Big day — overlapping celebrations!
Early morning: Breakfast with pan de muerto and hot chocolate at a local café. Quick stroll to see street decorations/altars in Roma before heading to the track.
Midday: Leave for Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez by 10:00–10:30 AM Denver / 11:00–11:30 AM local. Race starts 1:00 PM Denver / 2:00 PM local.
Evening: Return from race. If energy permits, catch part of the Día de Muertos parade on Paseo de la Reforma or stroll through Roma to take in the costumed crowds—streets will be packed with skeleton costumes and skull makeup!
November 2 (Monday) — Día de Muertos Continued
Morning: Leisurely recovery breakfast. Get calavera face painting by a street artist.
Afternoon: Visit National Museum of Anthropology for Día de Muertos exhibition (crowds smaller than Nov 1). Or explore Centro Histórico and Templo Mayor.
Late afternoon: Optional cemetery visit to see families honoring their loved ones with marigolds, candles, and photos. Peaceful, spiritual atmosphere.
Evening: Special Día de Muertos menu dinner. Many places have extended celebrations—last chance to fully immerse.
November 3 (Tuesday) — Departure Day
Morning: Pack and prepare for departure. Grab last pan de muerto or café de olla for the road if you have time.
Afternoon: Head to airport for your flight home to Denver.
💡 Día de Muertos Tips:
• It's not morbid or scary—it's a joyful celebration of life and remembrance
• Be respectful if visiting cemeteries—you're witnessing intimate family moments
• Photography is generally welcome but always ask permission before photographing people
• Wear comfortable shoes—you'll be walking a lot
• Nov 1–2 will be crowded in popular areas; plan accordingly
• Many restaurants and shops have special Día de Muertos menus and decorations—ask what's special
• This is a UNESCO-recognized Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity—you're experiencing something truly special!

🚗 Getting Around Mexico City

Transportation Options

🚕 Uber / Didi (Recommended)
Safest and most convenient
Both apps work great in Mexico City. Safer than street taxis. Download before arrival. Prices reasonable (40–200 MXN for short rides). Surge pricing during rush hours and after nightlife.
🚇 Metro (Subway) ↗
Cheap and efficient (20 MXN = ~$1)
13 lines covering the city. Fast, but crowded during rush hours (7–9 AM, 6–8 PM). Get Tarjeta Libre rechargeable card at stations. Safe during day and early evening; avoid late night.
🚌 Buses (Peseros)
Cheap (5–10 MXN) but complicated
Colorful local buses everywhere. Hard to navigate for tourists without Spanish. Ask hotel staff for help if needed. Good for short, local trips.
🚴 Ecobici (Bike Rental) ↗
Fun for exploring Roma/Condesa
Bike-sharing system throughout city. Around 300 MXN/day. Great for neighborhoods, but heavy traffic on main avenues. Best for leisurely day exploring.

Getting to/from Airport

Benito Juárez International Airport (MEX) ↗
📍 20 km northeast | 30–45 min from Roma
Best option: Uber/Didi (150–250 MXN = $8–14). Budget option: Airport metro line (~150 MXN, ~1 hour with connections). Premium: Pre-arranged car service (200–300 MXN).

Safety Tips

✅ Safe Areas (where you'll be): Roma, Condesa, Polanco, Chapultepec, Centro Histórico daytime
⚠️ General Rules: Avoid displaying expensive jewelry/phones. Don't walk alone at night (use Uber). Stay in well-lit, populated areas. Avoid the southern suburbs.
💡 Metro vs. Uber Decision: Use metro for long distances (cheap, time-predictable). Use Uber for convenient, safe point-to-point travel (especially evenings). Roma neighborhood is very walkable—many attractions are within 20 minutes on foot.

💡 Local Tips & Essentials

Currency: Mexican Peso (MXN). 1 USD ≈ 17–18 MXN.
ATMs: Plenty in Roma, withdraw pesos. Most charge ~20–50 MXN fee.

Mexico City sits at 2,250 m (7,382 ft). You may feel mild altitude effects first 1–2 days. Hydrate well, avoid heavy activity initially. Coca-Cola with lime is traditional remedy.
